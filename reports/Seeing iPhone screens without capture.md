# Give Mantiza eyes without screen recording

Mantiza has two realistic ways to see other apps without ScreenCaptureKit or Photos screenshots. **On the phone alone**, the best route is a Shortcuts "Take Screenshot" action that hands its image straight to a background Mantiza App Intent. The ESP32 can fire it through an AssistiveTouch action. The image never touches Photos, but each capture costs a screen flash and a Shortcuts banner. **When the Mac is reachable**, the best route is a developer-service video stream from the phone. pymobiledevice3's DisplayService runs over USB, or over Wi-Fi after one USB setup, and shows no red recording indicator. XCUITest/WebDriverAgent can add a full element tree (labels, frames, types) for any foreground app, at the cost of an "Automation Running" overlay. Everything else is a niche or a bet. iPhone Mirroring works only while the phone is locked and nobody is touching it. AirPlay into the Mac goes black under capture on macOS Tahoe. HDMI out through a capture dongle works but is bulky. A VoiceOver braille-display emulation on the M5StickS3 would be the most elegant structured channel if iOS accepts it, but two earlier BLE attempts failed and nobody has shown it working. Apple Intelligence on-screen awareness gives third-party apps nothing, because it only flows from an app to Siri, never back. None of the per-route latency numbers below have been measured on iOS 27. The recommended architecture is therefore built around about six cheap bench tests that decide the fallback order.

## The Shortcuts screenshot keeps pixels out of Photos but announces itself every time

The **"Take Screenshot" action returns its image only inside the shortcut.** Nothing lands in Photos unless a Save step follows it. iOS 26 users running a bare Take Screenshot from the Action button saw the screen flash, but no image appeared in their library until they added "Save to Photo Album" ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256213252)). That property is what makes it useful to Mantiza. The next action can be "Mantiza: Ingest Screenshot", taking an `IntentFile` parameter. Since iOS 26, `AppIntent.supportedModes` / `IntentModes` lets that intent declare **`.background`**, so it runs in Mantiza's process without bringing the app to the front ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/appintent/supportedmodes.md); [IntentModes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/intentmodes.md); [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/833681)). Mantiza already ships App Intents for Siri, Shortcuts and Control Center workflows, so the plumbing exists. The new pieces are the ingest intent and an App Group or Mac hand-off.

The UX cost is real. Users report **a screen flash** on every run ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256213252)). An older guide describes **a Shortcuts notification banner "for a second or two" that cannot be disabled**, though it found no corner thumbnail ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/720221/how-to-take-a-screenshot-on-iphone-without-a-thumbnail-preview/)). That guide dates from iOS 14/15, so the banner's current behaviour and whether it is burned into the returned image are both unknown. The iPhone 13 has a notch rather than a Dynamic Island, so the feedback probably shows as a top banner. If the banner is captured, Mantiza can mask the top band of the image. If it isn't, the cost is cosmetic. One third-party guide claims background intents are killed after about 30 seconds ([capgo](https://tanstack.com/intent/registry/%2540capgo%252Fcapgo-skills/capacitor-app-intents)). That doesn't matter for a single ingest, but it rules out a long-running loop inside one intent.

The trigger decides whether this route works for an autonomous agent. **Back Tap** can run a shortcut ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/111772)), but it needs a physical tap. The iPhone 13 has no Action button. **AssistiveTouch custom actions and menu items can be assigned a shortcut**, because "all your shortcuts are listed at the bottom" of the action list ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2022/07/22/use-assistivetouch-for-customizable-shortcuts-on-the-iphone-and-ipad/)). The M5StickS3 already drives AssistiveTouch, so it can always open the menu and click the item, at the cost of the menu appearing on screen and possibly in the frame. The faster, cleaner version maps an extra mouse button (button 4 or 5) in AssistiveTouch > Devices directly to the shortcut, so a single HID report from the stick fires a capture. That mapping is plausible but **unverified on iOS 27**. It is the most important test in this report, because it decides whether the phone-only route needs no visible menu at all. I found no source showing that a Bluetooth keyboard chord can run a user shortcut. Latency is estimated at **0.5–2 s per capture** with no measured figures. That is acceptable next to a vision-model step of several seconds, but too slow for continuous video.

Two adjacent Shortcuts actions help but cannot replace pixels. **"Get What's On Screen"** returns links or files only from apps that host shareable content, such as Safari's URL. Finder, Notes, Mail and Terminal returned nothing on the Mac ([Matthew Cassinelli](https://matthewcassinelli.com/actions/get-whats-on-screen/); [MacScripter](https://www.macscripter.net/t/the-get-whats-on-screen-shortcut-action/77716)). That makes it a cheap side signal. **Use Model** (iOS 26) runs a prompt over variables you pass in. No source says it reads the screen on its own ([Matthew Cassinelli](https://matthewcassinelli.com/ios-26-public-beta-shortcuts-actions-apple-intelligence-messages-notes-checklists/)). Chaining it after Take Screenshot would give a free on-device caption, but Mantiza already has Laya for that.

## Apple's on-screen awareness only flows into Siri, never out

The iOS 18–27 "on-screen awareness" APIs give **no third-party access to another app's screen**. `NSUserActivity.appEntityIdentifier` and iOS 27's SwiftUI `.appEntityIdentifier(_:)` modifier let an app expose *its own* entities to Siri ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/nsuseractivity/appentityidentifier.md)). One developer tested iOS 27 beta 3 and found Siri answering "describe this" from **its own automatic screenshot**. The app's `entities(for:)` was never called. The only cross-app transfer that worked was "send this to <app>" using the `.files.file` schema with a real file on disk. Apple DTS logged FB23813341 ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/838329)). WWDC 2026 coverage says Siri can interpret the screen "across third-party apps", but in that design apps contribute App Intents and do not receive the screen ([Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/2026-wwdc-ios-27-updates); [Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/3-things-for-developers-from-wwdc-2026)). The rest of the on-phone surface is closed too. DeviceActivity report extensions cannot send data out by design, and they only see usage data ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/834794)). Classroom Screen View works only on supervised iPads ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/classroom/id1085319084)). Live Text and Vision `RecognizeTextRequest` work only on images Mantiza already holds. They are the natural OCR stage after a Shortcut capture.

For completeness, iOS 27 replaced the Broadcast Extension with **SCContentSharingPicker + SCStream**, and a backgrounded stream needs `UIBackgroundModes: screen-capture` ([Apple sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/screencapturekit/capturing-screen-content-on-ios.md); [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/840821)). A developer-signed build can declare that background mode freely. Nothing documented avoids the indicator or the picker. No source says whether a picker choice persists across app launches, which is cheap to check once. That keeps it as the last resort the question already describes.

## The Mac sees best over a cable, and DisplayService may be silent

Every Mac route avoids iOS's red recording pill. **Each one changes something else visible on the phone**, and they differ sharply in whether the phone stays usable.

**pymobiledevice3 DisplayService** is the strongest candidate. On iOS 17+ it opens developer services through an in-process RSD tunnel with **no root**. It works over USB, or over Wi-Fi after a one-time `lockdown wifi-connections on` ([pymobiledevice3 tunnels guide](https://doronz88.github.io/pymobiledevice3/guides/ios17-tunnels/)). It exposes `display serve-web`, `serve-vnc` and `start-video-stream`, which give a continuous hardware-encoded stream rather than PNG round-trips. In v11.17.0, `developer core-device display serve-vnc` binds to localhost unless you pass `--bind 0.0.0.0` and `--password` ([release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/doronz88/pymobiledevice3/release/v11.17.0)). A Personal-Team phone should be able to enable Developer Mode after any Xcode install. Three things are unknown: whether DisplayService shows *any* on-phone indicator, whether its VNC server accepts pointer input, and its frame rate on Lightning. If it is both silent and controllable, it beats every other route in this report. The older DVT screenshot (`developer dvt screenshot`) still works on 17+ with a mounted Developer Disk Image ([skills.cat](https://skills.cat/skills/neversight/skills-feed/ios-device-screenshot)). It makes a good single-frame fallback, probably at hundreds of milliseconds per frame.

**AVFoundation `.muxed` capture over Lightning** is the documented wired path. A Mac app sets `kCMIOHardwarePropertyAllowScreenCaptureDevices` and the iPhone appears as a capture device ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremediaio/kcmiohardwarepropertyallowscreencapturedevices.md); [ctypes gist](https://gist.github.com/rgov/737a8388747d44f81e5792d89188859f)). It is QuickTime's mechanism. **No red dot appears**, but iOS has historically swapped in a clean "9:41, full battery" status bar on the device itself ([MakeTechEasier](https://maketecheasier.com/record-iphone-screen-with-quicktime); [iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/?p=310951)). Nobody has confirmed that behaviour on iOS 26/27. The agent loses the real clock and battery in its frames, which is a minor perception cost. Wired captures also appear as a normal "Display" source in recorders, so the Tahoe AirPlay black-out apparently doesn't affect them ([Tella](https://www.tella.com/docs/help/tips/i-phone-demos-using-screen-mirroring)).

**iPhone Mirroring** is the only no-hardware route that gives both pixels and control, but it inverts Mantiza's model. **The iPhone must stay locked.** Unlocking it ends the session, and there is no Face ID, camera or microphone ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120421)). The open-source servers mirroir-mcp and iphone-mirror-mcp already drive it, using window screenshots plus CGEvent input with only a single pointer. They report that **Control Center and hardware buttons are unreachable** and that DRM content captures black ([mirroir-mcp](https://github.com/jfarcand/mirroir-mcp); [iphone-mirror-mcp](https://github.com/nickatnight96/iphone-mirror-mcp)). Many users report "Connection Interrupted" and timeouts on 26.x ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256148409); [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/828526)). It is not available in the EU. It fits a separate "phone on the desk, Mac drives" mode, not the hold-and-use agent.

**AirPlay into the Mac's built-in receiver** should be dropped. On Tahoe, the mirrored window is reported to go **black as soon as any capture starts** ([Tella](https://www.tella.com/docs/help/tips/i-phone-demos-using-screen-mirroring); [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256155053)). The receiver has no input channel. It also blocks iPhone Mirroring, because the two are mutually exclusive ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120421)).

All Mac routes share one structural cost: **the Mac has to be present**, cabled or on a network where peer traffic works. Campus networks with client isolation can break Bonjour and TCP between the Mac and phone. Mantiza's firmware 0.4.4 Mac discovery already gives the app a natural "Mac eye available" signal, and the ESP32's BLE link is a fallback relay for small results.

## Small-device receivers work, but are bulky or fragile

**Wired HDMI out** mirrors the entire screen with no app involved. The phone encodes its output and the adapter's ARM SoC decodes it to HDMI, which adds lag and compression artifacts ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013/03/04/heres-how-apples-lightning-to-hdmi-digital-av-adapter-probably/)). The adapter tops out around 1080p ([listing](https://ibluetech.ca/en/product/lightning-digital-av-adapter)). DRM apps raise HDCP errors, but ordinary UI should pass ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT202044); [Apple Community](https://discussions-kr-prz.apple.com/thread/256138707)). An MS2109 dongle is a plain UVC webcam at 1080p30 MJPEG. One hobbyist measured roughly **100–120 ms local MJPEG latency** and 220–350 ms uncompressed on Linux ([fluxcoil](https://fluxcoil.net/wiki/hardwarerelated/camlink_hdmi_converter)). The **M5StickS3 cannot host such a dongle**. The ESP32-S3's USB is Full Speed only (about 1 MB/s bulk), and Espressif's UVC host lists 320x240@30 and 640x480@15 modes ([Espressif](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/usb_host_uvc/versions/1.0.2~1/readme)). The practical rig is adapter, then MS2109, then a Mac or Pi Zero 2 W, for an estimated $80–100. It takes the only Lightning port, so charging has to go through the adapter, and it is poor for a pocket. No source describes an on-phone indicator for wired mirroring. Its best use is as a ground-truth capture path for collecting training data.

**AirPlay receivers on a Pi or ESP32** mostly work on iOS 27, but fragility is built in. UxPlay (GPL-3.0, v1.74) runs on Pi Zero 2 W through Pi 5, with hardware H.264 decode only up to Pi 4B. The README says there is "no guarantee" Apple keeps the legacy mirroring protocol ([UxPlay](https://github.com/FDH2/UxPlay)). Issue #535 shows iOS 27 needing `-h265` or later fixes. The maintainer calls it "(probably?) fixed" ([UxPlay #535](https://github.com/FDH2/UxPlay/issues/535)). onehumanbeing/esp32-iphone-agent shows an ESP32-S3 can act as an AirPlay pass-through. It hosts its own hotspot, leaves the H.264 undecoded and relays it over BLE at **480x1040 and at most 5 fps**. The phone loses normal Wi-Fi, mirroring stops on lock, and passcode and Apple Pay screens are blanked ([onehumanbeing](https://github.com/onehumanbeing/esp32-iphone-agent)). Every AirPlay route also has to be started by hand from Control Center, because no public API starts mirroring. Miracast and Chromecast aren't native to iPhone, so they fall back to ReplayKit ([Alphr](https://www.alphr.com/mirror-iphone-with-chromecast)).

**Licensing is the hard line here.** The receiver code in onehumanbeing's `hardware/hid_bridge/src/airplay/` is ported from UxPlay and is **GPL-3.0**. Any firmware that links it becomes GPL-3.0. The rest of that repository is MIT ([onehumanbeing](https://github.com/onehumanbeing/esp32-iphone-agent)). Mantiza must not copy, port or "adapt" that directory or UxPlay source into the stick firmware. If it ever wants an on-stick receiver, it needs a clean-room implementation written from protocol documentation, by someone who has not been working from that code. Running an unmodified UxPlay binary as a separate program on the Mac for a bench test is a different situation from linking its code into Mantiza. Even so, the integration should stay at the process boundary. Before vendoring any code from the Mac-side tools (pymobiledevice3, go-ios, mirroir-mcp), check each project's license file. Calling them as external processes is the safer default.

## Accessibility gives structure, through a host or an unproven braille trick

**XCUITest/WebDriverAgent** gives the highest-fidelity non-pixel view: the foreground app's full accessibility tree, Springboard included, built with `XCUIApplication(bundleIdentifier:)` ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/joduchan/coordinate-based-ui-tests-break-so-we-read-the-accessibility-tree-instead-from-inside-the-3gl7)). One snapshot costs about the same as snapshotting the whole screen, and later queries are nearly free ([Appelium](https://developer.appelium.com/engineering/snapshot-query/)). The costs are a host and fragility. On iOS 17+, WDA "immediately stops when iPhone is disconnected" from the host ([Appium Discuss](https://discuss.appium.io/t/running-wda-on-ios-17/42392)). iOS shows an **"Automation Running" overlay** ([Appium troubleshooting](https://scriptagc.wasmer.app/https_appium_github_io/appium-xcuitest-driver/latest/guides/troubleshooting)). callstack's agent-device reports snapshots that "grind ~30s" on pathological screens, plus `kAXErrorIllegalArgument` failures ([agent-device #701](https://github.com/callstack/agent-device/issues/701)). One report has WDA auto-uninstalled on an iPhone 13 mini running iOS 18.6 ([TesterHome](https://testerhome.com/topics/42764)). go-ios can launch an already-installed runner from Linux, Windows or macOS once its tunnel is up ([go-ios](https://github.com/danielpaulus/go-ios)), so a Pi could stand in for the Mac. The Mantiza app can't open a test session itself, but it can call a same-phone WDA over localhost while a host holds the session. Free Personal Team profiles expire after 7 days, so the runner needs a weekly reinstall (general knowledge, not separately sourced). Use this as a **structure supplement on Mac-present runs**, not the main eye.

**VoiceOver braille-display emulation** is the moonshot that fits Mantiza's hardware best. Apple engineers point developers to the USB-IF HID Braille page, **usage page 0x41 (HUTRR78)** ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/740301)). VoiceOver sends the display "the entire line where the VoiceOver cursor is focused" ([Apple VoiceOver guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/voiceover/vo15607/mac)). Braille key commands cover next and previous item, activate, Home, scroll, toggle speech, mute and screen curtain ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-asia/118665)). In principle the M5StickS3 could walk every element and read each label over BLE, with no pixels, no Mac and VoiceOver silenced. In practice, **two nRF52840 BLE attempts were "not recognized"** on iOS 12 and 14 ([Nordic DevZone 43602](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/43602/ble-hid-braille-on-ios); [88287](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/88287/braille-display-not-correctly-work-on-ios-14)). Working HID braille on iOS has mostly been USB ([AppleVis](https://applevis.com/guides/using-braille-display-ios-introduction)). The output carries no element frames. Walking a screen is estimated at 2–10 s for 50 elements. VoiceOver also changes the touch model, and how an AssistiveTouch pointer behaves under VoiceOver is undocumented ([AppleVis forum](https://www.applevis.com/forum/voice-over-and-assistive-touch)). The only ESP32 sketch, brailletouch/HID-braille-ESP32, is **GPL-3.0**, untested on iOS, and appears to use the wrong cell usage ([GitHub](https://github.com/brailletouch/HID-braille-ESP32)). Write the descriptor from the USB-IF HUTRR78 specification itself and do not copy that sketch. NVDA and BRLTTY are GPL-family projects too, so treat them as reading material, not as a source of code. Voice Control, Switch Control, Full Keyboard Access and Dwell have **no output channel** at all. They are useful for actuation only.

## Recommended architecture: two eyes, one pair of hands

Keep the **M5StickS3 as the single actuator** in every mode. It is the only input path that works while the user holds an unlocked phone. Then put an "eye broker" in the app that picks the best frame source per step. The order is as follows. **First**, if Mac discovery finds Laya *and* a DisplayService (or `.muxed`) stream is live, use the Mac stream for continuous frames. Run the vision model on the Mac and send only actions and status back to the app over the existing link. **Second**, on Mac-present runs that need precise targets, take an XCUITest snapshot of the foreground app and merge its frames and labels with the pixels. Skip this when the overlay or the 30 s stalls would hurt. **Third**, the phone-only default is the Shortcut capture fired by a stick mouse button. The image goes into a `.background` App Intent, is cropped or masked, then OCR'd on-device with Vision, and is sent to Laya or the on-phone model. **Fourth**, if the button mapping fails, the stick opens the AssistiveTouch menu and clicks the shortcut item, and Mantiza masks the menu out of the frame. **Fifth**, ScreenCaptureKit stays a user-chosen "live mode" for long sessions where the indicator is acceptable. **iPhone Mirroring** becomes a separate unattended "desk mode". **HDMI** is kept for collecting training data. **The braille channel** stays a research branch that would replace the third and fourth tiers if it works.

|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Map AssistiveTouch pointer button 4/5 to that shortcut. Have the stick send the click while another app is in front | Zero-menu, stick-fired capture | Small firmware change |
| 3 | `pymobiledevice3 developer core-device display serve-vnc` on the iPhone 13 over USB, then Wi-Fi. Note fps, any on-phone indicator, and whether VNC clicks register | Mac eye (and maybe Mac hands) without any indicator | 1 hour on the Mac |

## Conclusion

The question changes once you stop treating "seeing the screen" as one capability. Mantiza needs a **phone-only snapshot path**, which Shortcuts supplies at the cost of a flash and banner, and a **Mac-present continuous path**, which developer services supply at the cost of a cable or a friendly network. Apple's AI features don't remove the need for either, because on iOS 27 they deliberately keep the screen inside Siri. The highest-leverage unknowns are cheap. A mouse-button mapping and a `serve-vnc` command, both testable in an afternoon, decide whether Mantiza's eyes are nearly invisible or merely tolerable.

A second takeaway is about durability rather than speed. Every route that looks attractive today depends on undocumented or unofficial behaviour: Shortcuts' banner policy, DisplayService, the legacy AirPlay protocol, BLE braille acceptance. The eye broker should therefore be a first-class, version-tested module that re-runs the go/no-go suite after each iOS point release. Anything that might put GPL code into the stick firmware should stay behind a process boundary or a clean-room rewrite.
